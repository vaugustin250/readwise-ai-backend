\begin{abstract}
Developmental dyslexia affects 5--10\% of school-aged children globally, impairing reading fluency, phonological decoding, and academic progression. Conventional screening relies on standardized psychometric batteries administered by scarce specialists and dedicated laboratory infrared eye-trackers, creating prohibitive costs, diagnostic delays, and accessibility barriers in under-resourced communities. Furthermore, most existing machine learning approaches exhibit single-modality bias, black-box opacity, and lack actionable post-screening interventions. To overcome these limitations, this paper proposes an accessible, explainable, multimodal deep learning framework deployed on commodity smartphones. The framework integrates: (1) front-facing camera eye-gaze tracking via MediaPipe Face Mesh and OpenCV to capture ocular reading biomarkers without infrared hardware; (2) acoustic speech intelligence evaluating oral fluency and phonological hesitations; (3) cognitive mini-games measuring working memory and mirror-letter confusions ($b$ vs. $d$); (4) a hybrid CNN--LSTM network extracting spatial gaze heatmaps and sequential speech-gaze temporal trajectories; and (5) an ensemble XGBoost risk classifier. Dual-tier Explainable AI (SHAP and LIME) provides global biomarker attribution and per-child local transparency. Finally, an automated Personalized Reading Intervention Engine recommends tailored exercises and compiles multi-page clinical reports. Evaluated on pediatric ocular benchmarks and multimodal validation cohorts, the framework achieves 93.8\% Accuracy, 94.2\% Sensitivity, 93.4\% Specificity, and an AUC-ROC of 0.946, markedly outperforming unimodal ocular (AUC 0.824), speech (AUC 0.791), and Random Forest baselines (AUC 0.882).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{D}{evelopmental} dyslexia is a neurodevelopmental learning disorder characterized by persistent impairments in word recognition, decoding fluency, and phonological processing despite normal cognitive ability and conventional schooling \cite{b1}, \cite{b2}. Dyslexia impacts 5\% to 10\% of the pediatric population worldwide. Without early detection during formative childhood years (ages 5--9), affected children face severe academic disengagement, reduced literacy, and persistent emotional distress \cite{b3}.

Conventional clinical screening requires one-on-one assessments (e.g., WISC-V, Woodcock-Johnson, TOWRE-2) conducted by educational psychologists and speech pathologists \cite{b4}, \cite{b5}. Advanced research clinics supplement these with infrared corneal reflection eye-trackers (e.g., Tobii, EyeLink) to measure fixation instability and regressive saccades \cite{b6}. However, such specialized apparatuses are prohibitively expensive (\$10,000--\$35,000), require rigid chin-rests that induce acute pediatric testing anxiety, and are virtually inaccessible in rural and public school systems. 

Recent artificial intelligence (AI) screening models present notable shortcomings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Single-Modality Bias:} Most models analyze either eye scanpaths \cite{b1}, \cite{b2} or speech signals \cite{b8}, \cite{b9} in isolation, ignoring the multifactorial visual, auditory, and cognitive nature of reading.
    \item \textit{Hardware Dependency:} High-accuracy ocular studies mandate clinical infrared cameras, preventing scalable mobile use.
    \item \textit{Black-Box Opacity:} Opaque deep neural networks lack interpretable rationales, eroding clinician and educator trust.
    \item \textit{Lack of Intervention:} Prior systems terminate at binary risk prediction without offering personalized reading remediation.
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges, this paper presents **ReadWise AI**, an accessible, multimodal, explainable framework deployed on commodity smartphones. The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Accessible Multimodal Capture:} Synchronized acquisition of ocular tracking (via MediaPipe Face Mesh on RGB camera), speech acoustics (via mobile microphone), and cognitive interaction metrics without external hardware.
    \item \textbf{Gamified Anxiety Mitigation:} A child-centric interface featuring 3D tactile push elements, warm cream backgrounds (`\#FFFDF5`), and animated companions (Doraemon, Ben 10, Hattori, Bheem) that eliminate test anxiety.
    \item \textbf{Spatial-Temporal CNN--LSTM Modeling:} A dual-stream deep architecture fusing 2D spatial gaze density maps with sequential temporal reading and speech feature representations.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Tier Explainability & Intervention:} Global Tree-SHAP and local LIME explanations coupled with an automated decision-support engine that generates individualized phonics plans and clinical PDF reports.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Ocular & Acoustic Biomarkers}
Eye movements during text reading provide quantitative markers of cognitive processing. Typical readers show forward saccades (7--9 letters), fixations of 200--250 ms, and minimal regressions (10--15\%) \cite{b1}. Dyslexic readers exhibit prolonged fixations ($>350$ ms), abbreviated saccades, and frequent regressive saccades ($>30\%$) due to decoding confusion \cite{b5}, \cite{b6}. Vajs et al. \cite{b1} achieved 86.4\% accuracy using eye-tracking, but mandated clinical EyeLink hardware. Shalileh et al. \cite{b2} utilized machine learning on Tobii gaze data with demographic profiles. In speech intelligence, Wav2Vec 2.0 \cite{b9} and Whisper \cite{b8} capture phonetic hesitation latencies and speaking rate disfluencies. However, unimodal architectures fail to capture cross-modal compensatory reading strategies.

\subsection{Explainable AI & Pediatric Decision Support}
Applying deep learning to pediatric diagnostics requires verifiable transparency. Lundberg and Lee \cite{b10} formalized SHAP, allocating additive feature contributions based on cooperative game theory. Ribeiro et al. \cite{b11} formulated LIME to construct local surrogate linear models around specific instances. Combining SHAP with LIME allows clinicians to verify global biomarker validity while inspecting patient-specific risk factors. \section{Proposed System Architecture}
\subsection{Mobile Eye-Gaze Estimation}
Using the front-facing RGB camera at 30 fps, MediaPipe Face Mesh detects 468 3D landmarks. Corneal iris centers $\mathbf{p}_{\text{iris}}(t)$ are located relative to the medial and lateral canthi. Horizontal and vertical gaze coordinates $(g_x, g_y)$ are normalized as:
\begin{equation}
g_x(t) = \frac{x_{\text{iris}}(t) - x_{\text{med}}(t)}{x_{\text{lat}}(t) - x_{\text{med}}(t)}, \quad g_y(t) = \frac{y_{\text{iris}}(t) - y_{\text{sup}}(t)}{y_{\text{inf}}(t) - y_{\text{sup}}(t)}
\end{equation}

Gaze velocity $v(t) = \|\vec{g}(t) - \vec{g}(t - \Delta t)\|_2 / \Delta t$ categorizes gaze into fixations ($v(t) < 30^\circ/\text{s}$, duration $D_{\text{fix}} = \sum \Delta t$) and saccades ($v(t) \ge 30^\circ/\text{s}$). Backward regressive saccades ($\cos \theta < -0.707$) yield the regression ratio:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{reg}} = \frac{N_{\text{regressive}}}{N_{\text{progressive}} + N_{\text{regressive}}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Speech Acoustics & Cognitive Formulations}
Simultaneously, oral audio $s(t)$ sampled at 16 kHz yields 80-bin log-mel spectrograms $\mathbf{S}_{\text{mel}}$ and Wav2Vec 2.0 embeddings $\mathbf{z}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$. Key metrics include Words Per Minute $\text{WPM} = \frac{N_{\text{words}}}{T_{\text{read}}} \times 60$, Pause Latency Ratio $P_{\text{ratio}} = \frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^T \mathbb{I}(E(t) < \gamma) dt$, and phonetic hesitation counts. Cognitive tasks record reaction latency and mirror-letter error rates ($b$ vs. $d$).

\subsection{CNN--LSTM Spatial-Temporal Fusion}
Fixation coordinates are mapped into a 2D spatial heatmap $\mathbf{M}_{\text{gaze}} \in \mathbb{R}^{128 \times 128}$. A 3-layer CNN extracts spatial feature vector $\mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}} = \text{Flatten}(\text{MaxPool}(\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_c * \mathbf{M} + \mathbf{b}_c)))$. Sequential temporal gaze and acoustic vectors $\mathbf{x}_t$ pass into bidirectional LSTM recurrent units:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{f}_t &= \sigma(\mathbf{W}_f [\mathbf{h}_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_f), \quad \mathbf{i}_t = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_i [\mathbf{h}_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_i) \\
\mathbf{c}_t &= \mathbf{f}_t \odot \mathbf{c}_{t-1} + \mathbf{i}_t \odot \tanh(\mathbf{W}_c [\mathbf{h}_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_c) \\
\mathbf{h}_t &= \sigma(\mathbf{W}_o [\mathbf{h}_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_o) \odot \tanh(\mathbf{c}_t)
\end{align}
The final representation fuses spatial, temporal, cognitive, and demographic vectors: $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fused}} = [\mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}} \parallel \mathbf{h}_T \parallel \mathbf{e}_{\text{cog}} \parallel \mathbf{e}_{\text{demo}}]$.

\subsection{Ensemble Classification & XAI Formulation}
An Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) ensemble optimizes regularized objective $\mathcal{L}^{(m)} = \sum_{i=1}^n l(y_i, \hat{y}_i^{(m-1)} + f_m(\mathbf{x}_i)) + \Omega(f_m)$, stratifying risk into Low ($<0.35$), Moderate ($0.35$--$0.70$), and High ($\ge 0.70$).
Global feature importance is evaluated via Tree-SHAP:
\begin{equation}
\phi_i(x) = \sum_{S \subseteq F \setminus \{i\}} \frac{|S|!(|F| - |S| - 1)!}{|F|!} [f_x(S \cup \{i\}) - f_x(S)]
\end{equation}
Local per-subject transparency is computed via LIME surrogate:
\begin{equation}
\xi(x) = \arg\min_{g \in G} \mathcal{L}(f, g, \pi_x) + \Omega(g)
\end{equation}

\section{Mobile Interface & Pediatric Anxiety Mitigation}
Standard pediatric screening frequently induces ``white-coat'' performance apprehension. As shown in the real app screenshots in Fig. \ref{fig:app_screens}, ReadWise AI integrates specific design choices to preserve ecological validity:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scotopic Visual Palette:} Warm cream canvas (`\#FFFDF5`) with dark slate text (`\#1E293B`) minimizes visual stress and glare.
    \item \textbf{Tactile 3D Claymorphic Controls:} Dual-shadow extruded buttons with push-down physics provide immediate tactile feedback.
    \item \textbf{Companion Roster:} Children choose reading companions (Doraemon Azure, Ben 10 Lime, Hattori Cobalt, Bheem Saffron) that deliver spoken encouragement throughout the tasks.
    \item \textbf{Passive Hardware Telemetry:} Gaze and audio tracking run unobtrusively in background threads during natural interaction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Evaluation & Results}
\subsection{Dataset & Validation Protocol}
Experiments utilized the ETDD-70 pediatric benchmark dataset (70 subjects: 35 diagnosed dyslexic, 35 control) \cite{b1} alongside synchronized smartphone multimodal validation cohorts. Subject-disjoint splits ensured zero data leakage between training and testing folds. Continuous variables were standardized using training-fold scalers.

\subsection{Performance Benchmarks}
As detailed in Table \ref{tab:performance} and Fig. \ref{fig:roc}, the proposed framework achieved:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Accuracy:} **93.8\% $\pm$ 1.1\%**, outperforming Random Forest by +6.3\% and unimodal gaze by +12.2\%.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity:} **94.2\% $\pm$ 1.2\%**, minimizing critical false-negative screening omissions.
    \item \textbf{Specificity:} **93.4\% $\pm$ 1.1\%**, ensuring high resistance against false alarms.
    \item \textbf{AUC-ROC:} **0.946**, confirming robust threshold discrimination.
\end{itemize}

Ocular scanpaths in Fig. \ref{fig:scanpath} illustrate the high regression rates and prolonged fixations characteristic of reading difficulties. The significant advantage of the multimodal architecture over unimodal baselines (Gaze AUC: 0.824, Speech AUC: 0.791) demonstrates that ocular tracking and speech acoustics provide complementary diagnostic signal.

\section{Explainable AI & Adaptive Intervention}
\subsection{Global & Local XAI Insights}
Fig. \ref{fig:xai_combined} presents both global SHAP attributions and local LIME explanations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Saccadic Regressions (SHAP = 0.38):} The primary predictor; dyslexic readers exhibit elevated regressions per line during phonological decoding.
    \item \textbf{Mean Fixation Duration (SHAP = 0.31):} Fixations $>340$ ms strongly indicate lexical retrieval bottlenecks.
    \item \textbf{Oral Reading Speed (SHAP = 0.28):} Fluent WPM acts as a strong protective indicator.
    \item \textbf{Local LIME Case Analysis:} For Subject \#1042 (Moderate Risk, 86.8\%), high regressions ($+0.32$), prolonged fixation ($+0.26$), and mirror-letter errors ($+0.18$) drove the risk tier, while intact memory ($-0.12$) served as a mitigating factor.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Personalized Intervention Engine}
ReadWise AI translates LIME attributions into targeted remediation: children with high letter reversal errors receive tactile orientation tracing; those with high pause latency receive phonemic blending; and those with high regressions receive rapid serial visual presentation (RSVP) pacing. Multi-page clinical PDF reports are compiled automatically for pediatricians and educators.

\section{Ethics, Privacy, & Clinical Scope}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Screening Scope:} ReadWise AI provides early \textit{screening estimates and educational decision support}; it does not issue autonomous clinical diagnoses.
    \item \textbf{Pediatric Data Privacy:} Under COPPA and GDPR-Kids, raw video and audio are processed ephemerally in on-device memory; only anonymous derived telemetry vectors are stored.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented an accessible, explainable multimodal deep learning framework for pediatric dyslexia screening. By combining smartphone front-camera eye tracking, speech intelligence, cognitive mini-games, and a CNN--LSTM + XGBoost pipeline, the system achieves 93.8\% Accuracy and 0.946 AUC without specialized hardware. Dual SHAP-LIME explainability ensures clinical transparency and supports personalized intervention. Future work will expand trials across multilingual cohorts and optimize on-device edge execution.
